# Animations in pages: plan for 0.7.0

Asked for on 2026-11-26. This is a plan; nothing is built yet.

The owner wants to draw something simple in several steps, put it in a page, and show it in three ways: as steps to click through, as a short video that plays once, or as a loop like a GIF. The example is the SRP pathway in seven steps. The membrane, the labels and the SRP receptor stay in the same place throughout, and only the ribosome, the chain and the SRP change.

## Which release

**0.7.0, the first release after 0.6.** 0.6.7 hardens and releases the 0.6 line and adds no features. An animation is a new kind of block with a new drawing format, so it should not land in a hardening release. It starts the 0.7 line. Because 0.7.0 ends in `.0`, its release notes have two parts (since 0.6.7, and since 0.6.0).

## How it works for the person

### Making one

- Type **/animation** in a page, just like **/canvas**. A new animation block appears in the text, showing an empty box. The canvas opens next to the text (the split view), with the view on the animation's box and the step bar under it.
- The animation is a box on the page's canvas, like a canvas region. It is 600 × 340 at first and can be resized from its corners. All steps share the same box.
- **The step bar** sits under the box while the animation is being edited:
  - **◀ Step 3 of 7 ▶** moves between steps. The `,` and `.` keys do the same, so the step can change without moving the pen.
  - **Duplicate step** puts a copy of this step right after it and goes to the copy. This is the "copy to the next frame and erase what you don't need" the owner asked for.
  - **New step** adds an empty step after this one.
  - **Move left** and **Move right** change a step's place, and **Delete step** removes it (Undo brings it back).
  - **Caption**: a short title and a line of text for the step, for example "4. Dock at the ER, elongation halts" with its sentence. The caption is shown under the animation in step mode.
  - **Background (all steps)** is a switch. While it is on, everything drawn goes on a layer that every step shows: the membranes, "Cytosol", "ER lumen", the SRP receptor. Fixing a label there fixes it in all seven steps at once. With the switch off, drawing goes into the current step only. Duplicate step is still there for things that move a little from step to step.
- **Seeing the steps before and after** (onion skin): a switch on the step bar, off by default. The step before shows in a faint cool tint and the step after in a faint warm tint, both see-through. They are only guides: they cannot be selected or erased, and they are never printed or exported. The switch is remembered on the device.
- Every drawing tool works inside the box: pen, shapes, arrows, text notes, eraser and loop select. The eraser and loop select only touch the current step (or the background, when that switch is on). Drawing outside the box goes onto the page canvas as before.
- Outside editing, the box on the canvas shows step 1 with a small label: "Animation, 7 steps".

### Showing it in the page

- The block shows the animation in one of three ways:
  - **Steps** (the default): **Back** and **Next** buttons, dots for the steps, and the caption under the drawing. This is exactly the layout of the owner's example. The arrow keys work when the block has focus.
  - **Video**: a play button. It plays once at the set speed and stops on the last step. Clicking while it plays pauses it, and clicking after the end plays it again.
  - **Loop (GIF)**: it plays over and over while it is on screen. It stops when scrolled out of view, when the window is hidden, or while the page is being edited around it. If the system asks for reduced motion, it waits for a click on play instead of starting by itself.
- **Right-click the block** (long-press on a phone or tablet):
  - **Show as**: Steps / Video / Loop (GIF), with a mark at the current choice.
  - **Speed**: 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 or 24 steps per second, or **Other…** for any number from 0.5 to 30. This is only offered for Video and Loop. The default is 2, because these animations have few steps.
  - **Edit in canvas**, and **Remove from page** (the animation stays on the canvas, like a canvas region).
- **Printing and Save as PDF** show every step one after another, small, each with its caption. That makes it a study sheet. A video or loop cannot play on paper, so all its steps are shown too.

## How it is stored

- **The drawing is in the page's own drawing file** (`Page.canvas.json`), like canvas regions. The page and its animations then sync, keep history, move and go to the trash together. Nothing else has to learn a new kind of file.
- A new list `animations` in the drawing file, written only when a page has an animation (format 7). Each animation has:
  - `id`, its box (`x`, `y`, `w`, `h`) and a `label`;
  - `background`: the shared layer, a list of items in the same form as the page's items;
  - `steps`: a list of `{ id, title, text, items }`.
  
  Items are stored relative to the box's corner, so moving the box moves every step with it.
- **The block in the markdown** is a fenced block, like the canvas block, with the display choices in it:

  ````
  ```scalenote-animation
  id: k3j9x2ab
  show: steps
  fps: 2
  ```
  ````

  The way it is shown belongs to the block, not to the drawing, so the same animation can be shown as steps in one place and as a loop in another. Obsidian and other editors show the fenced block as plain text, which loses nothing. Lines the app does not know are kept byte for byte.
- **Older apps**: an app before 0.7.0 reads a format 7 drawing file as one it does not understand. It then saves nothing over it (the rule since format 6), so nothing is lost. That app can show the page's text but not its drawing. The release notes say to update every device first, as 0.6 did for offline choices.
- **Limits** (checked when the file is read, like everything else in drawing files): at most 50 animations per page, 300 steps per animation, a title of 120 characters and a text of 1000 characters per step, and the existing limits on items and points for the whole file.

## How it is drawn

- The existing painter (`canvaspaint.js`) draws a step: the background, then the step's items, clipped to the box. Nothing new is needed for the drawing itself.
- For Video and Loop, each step is painted once into an off-screen picture, and those pictures are shown in turn. This keeps 24 steps a second smooth on a phone. The pictures are made again when the drawing changes or the theme switches.
- Only one timer runs per block, and only while that block is visible.

## What is not in 0.7.0 (later, if wanted)

- Movement worked out between two steps (tweening). Every step is drawn by hand. That matches "really simple stuff" and keeps the format small.
- A different length per step ("hold this step 2 seconds").
- Saving an animation as a GIF or video file, for use outside ScaleNote.
- Sound.

## Build plan in slices

| # | Slice | How it is checked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Format 7.** `animations` in the drawing file: reading with every check and limit, writing only when there are animations, items relative to the box, Undo entries for steps. | Unit tests that call the real `canvasrules.js` functions: reading back, bad and huge files refused, older formats unchanged, an older app's rule (unknown format, nothing saved). |
| 2 | **/animation and the block.** The slash command, the fenced block (with unknown lines kept), step 1 shown in the text, a calm message when the animation is gone. | Unit tests for reading and writing the fence; browser tests for /animation, reopening, and the fence in the markdown view. |
| 3 | **Editing on the canvas.** The box, the step bar (move between steps, duplicate, new, move, delete, caption), drawing and erasing in the current step only, the Background switch, the `,` and `.` keys. | Browser tests: duplicate then erase changes only that step, background shows in every step, Undo and Redo of every step action, a resize keeps every step in place. |
| 4 | **Before and after.** The onion-skin switch, tints from the theme, never printed or exported, remembered per device. | Browser tests and screenshots in light and dark. |
| 5 | **Showing it.** Steps, Video and Loop; the right-click and long-press menu with Show as and Speed; pausing when out of view or hidden; reduced motion; a cached picture per step. | Browser tests with a controlled clock: the right step at each tick, the loop stops when scrolled away, the menu writes `show:` and `fps:` into the block. |
| 6 | **Paper and wrap-up.** All steps with captions when printing and in Save as PDF, history and sync of a page with an animation, the design check, the security checklist, two-part release notes, version 0.7.0. | Full test runs; screenshots on a computer and a phone in light and dark; the owner draws the SRP example on the tablet. |
